# Causal Structure of Global Warming

[日本語版はこちら / Japanese version](CAUSAL_STRUCTURE_ja.md)

*A node-based map of the causal relationships between CO₂ emissions, carbon fixation collapse, natural circulation breakdown, and Earth metabolism decline.*

---

## Overview

Global warming is commonly modeled as a linear chain:

```
Human activity → CO₂ emissions → Greenhouse effect → Warming
```

This model is not wrong, but it is structurally incomplete.

A more accurate causal model must include:

- how carbon is absorbed, fixed, stored, and circulated by Earth systems
- how those systems degrade under human pressure and heat stress
- how their degradation feeds back into further warming

The causal structure described in this document treats global warming as an emergent property of a multi-node system — not a single-variable problem.

## Node Catalog

Each node represents a component of the Earth system relevant to carbon cycling, heat balance, or climate stability.

### Emission Nodes

| Node ID | Node Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| E-1 | Fossil Fuel Combustion | CO₂ and CH₄ from coal, oil, and natural gas |
| E-2 | Deforestation | Release of stored carbon through forest clearing and burning |
| E-3 | Agricultural Land Use | CH₄ from livestock, N₂O from fertilizers, soil carbon loss |
| E-4 | Industrial Processes | Cement, steel, chemical production CO₂ |
| E-5 | Waste and Incineration | CO₂ and CH₄ from landfills, open burning, food waste |

### Atmospheric and Radiative Nodes

| Node ID | Node Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| A-1 | Atmospheric CO₂ Concentration | The net balance between emissions and absorption |
| A-2 | Greenhouse Forcing | Radiative forcing from CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, and water vapor |
| A-3 | Surface Temperature | Global mean and regional surface temperature |
| A-4 | Ocean Heat Content | Thermal energy stored in the upper and deep ocean |
| A-5 | Precipitation and Drought Patterns | Shifts in regional water availability |

### Carbon Fixation Nodes

| Node ID | Node Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| F-1 | Forest Carbon Fixation | Photosynthetic CO₂ absorption and biomass storage in forests |
| F-2 | Soil Carbon Retention | Storage of organic carbon in mineral soil and humus layers |
| F-3 | Soil Microbial Activity | Decomposition, carbon cycling, and nutrient regeneration in soil |
| F-4 | Wetland Carbon Storage | Carbon sequestration in peatlands, mangroves, and marshes |
| F-5 | Phytoplankton Carbon Fixation | Oceanic primary production and biological carbon pump |
| F-6 | Seagrass and Seaweed | Coastal marine carbon fixation and storage |
| F-7 | Agricultural Organic Matter | Humus formation, compost cycling, crop residue management |

### Natural Circulation Nodes

| Node ID | Node Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| C-1 | Global Carbon Cycle | The integrated movement of carbon through atmosphere, biosphere, and lithosphere |
| C-2 | Water Cycle | Evaporation, precipitation, infiltration, and runoff |
| C-3 | Ocean Thermohaline Circulation | Deep ocean circulation driven by temperature and salinity gradients |
| C-4 | Nutrient Cycling | Cycling of nitrogen, phosphorus, and minerals through soil, water, and organisms |
| C-5 | Atmospheric Heat Distribution | Meridional transport of heat from tropics to poles |

### Earth Metabolism Nodes

| Node ID | Node Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| M-1 | Integrated Carbon Absorption Capacity | The combined ability of all fixation systems to remove atmospheric carbon |
| M-2 | Biodiversity and Ecosystem Function | Species diversity as a driver of ecological resilience and carbon cycling |
| M-3 | Soil Health Index | Composite measure of microbial activity, organic matter, and water retention |
| M-4 | Ocean Productivity | Combined phytoplankton biomass, carbon export, and biological pump efficiency |
| M-5 | Earth Metabolic Rate | Hypothetical aggregate measure of the planet's ability to process carbon and distribute heat |

### Anthropogenic Pressure Nodes

| Node ID | Node Name | Description |
|---|---|---|
| P-1 | Deforestation Rate | Annual area of forest cleared globally |
| P-2 | Soil Degradation | Loss of topsoil, microbial decline, compaction from industrial agriculture |
| P-3 | Ocean Pollution and Acidification | Chemical and thermal stress on marine ecosystems |
| P-4 | Wetland Destruction | Drainage and conversion of peatlands and coastal wetlands |
| P-5 | Urban Heat Island Effect | Local surface warming from concrete, reduced vegetation, and waste heat |
| P-6 | Chemical Input to Ecosystems | Pesticide, herbicide, and synthetic fertilizer effects on soil microbiome |

## Causal Relationship Map

### Primary Emission Pathway

```
E-1, E-2, E-3, E-4, E-5
        ↓
       A-1  (Atmospheric CO₂ rises)
        ↓
       A-2  (Greenhouse forcing increases)
        ↓
       A-3  (Surface temperature rises)
       A-4  (Ocean heat content rises)
```

### Carbon Fixation Collapse Pathway

```
P-1 (Deforestation) ──────────────────────→ F-1 ↓ (Forest fixation declines)
P-2 (Soil degradation) ───────────────────→ F-2 ↓, F-3 ↓ (Soil carbon and microbes decline)
P-3 (Ocean pollution/acidification) ──────→ F-5 ↓ (Phytoplankton decline)
P-4 (Wetland destruction) ────────────────→ F-4 ↓ (Wetland carbon storage collapses)
P-6 (Chemical inputs) ────────────────────→ F-3 ↓, F-7 ↓ (Microbial and agricultural carbon declines)

F-1 ↓, F-2 ↓, F-3 ↓, F-4 ↓, F-5 ↓, F-6 ↓, F-7 ↓
        ↓
       M-1 ↓  (Integrated carbon absorption capacity declines)
        ↓
       A-1 ↑  (CO₂ retention in atmosphere increases even without emission growth)
```

### Natural Circulation Breakdown Pathway

```
A-3 ↑ (Warming) ──────────────────────────→ C-2 disruption (altered precipitation, drought)
A-4 ↑ (Ocean heat) ───────────────────────→ C-3 weakening (thermohaline slowdown)
P-1, P-2 ─────────────────────────────────→ C-2 disruption (reduced forest evapotranspiration)
P-3 ──────────────────────────────────────→ C-4 disruption (nutrient cycle interference)

C-2 ↓, C-3 ↓, C-4 ↓
        ↓
   F-1, F-3, F-5 further decline (circulation-dependent fixation weakens)
        ↓
       M-1 ↓ (further decline in absorption capacity)
```

### Earth Metabolism Decline Pathway

```
M-1 ↓ + C-2 ↓ + C-3 ↓ + M-2 ↓ + M-3 ↓ + M-4 ↓
        ↓
       M-5 ↓  (Earth metabolic rate declines)
        ↓
   Planet loses capacity to self-regulate carbon balance
        ↓
       A-1 ↑  (CO₂ accumulates faster than natural systems can process)
```

## Feedback Loops

### Feedback Loop 1: Warming → Forest Stress → Fixation Decline → More Warming

```
A-3 ↑ → heat stress, drought on forests
        → F-1 ↓ (reduced photosynthesis, forest dieback)
        → M-1 ↓
        → A-1 ↑
        → A-2 ↑
        → A-3 ↑ (loop reinforces itself)
```

**Type:** Positive feedback (amplifying)
**Current evidence:** Amazon dieback, boreal forest die-off events, Mediterranean forest fires

---

### Feedback Loop 2: Ocean Warming → Phytoplankton Decline → Reduced Marine Carbon Pump

```
A-4 ↑ → ocean surface warming and stratification
        → F-5 ↓ (phytoplankton decline from nutrient limitation)
        → M-4 ↓ (biological pump weakens)
        → A-1 ↑
        → A-2 ↑
        → A-3 ↑, A-4 ↑ (loop reinforces)
```

**Type:** Positive feedback (amplifying)
**Current evidence:** Declining phytoplankton biomass in subtropical gyres (Boyce et al. 2010 and follow-up studies)

---

### Feedback Loop 3: Soil Warming → Microbial Respiration → Soil Carbon Release

```
A-3 ↑ → soil temperature rises
        → F-3 acceleration (microbial decomposition of soil organic matter increases)
        → F-2 ↓ (soil carbon stock declines)
        → A-1 ↑
        → A-3 ↑ (loop reinforces)
```

**Type:** Positive feedback (amplifying)
**Current evidence:** Permafrost thaw carbon release, tropical soil respiration observations

---

### Feedback Loop 4: Circulation Breakdown → Reduced Nutrient Supply → Fixation Decline

```
C-3 ↓ (weakened thermohaline circulation)
        → reduced upwelling of deep, nutrient-rich water
        → F-5 ↓ (phytoplankton nutrient limitation)
        → M-4 ↓
        → A-1 ↑ → A-3 ↑ → C-3 further weakening
```

**Type:** Positive feedback (amplifying)
**Current evidence:** AMOC slowdown observations, regional upwelling changes

---

### Stabilizing Loop (Natural Regulation, Currently Weakening)

```
A-1 ↑ → enhanced photosynthesis in some vegetation types (CO₂ fertilization effect)
        → F-1 partial increase
        → A-1 partial decrease
```

**Type:** Negative feedback (stabilizing)
**Caveat:** The CO₂ fertilization effect is limited by water and nutrient availability. Under drought and soil degradation conditions, this loop weakens significantly. It does not offset the net decline in fixation capacity at current warming levels.

## Comparative Causal Models

### Model 1: Single-Cause Linear Model (Common Understanding)

```
Human activity
→ CO₂ emissions increase
→ Greenhouse effect strengthens
→ Earth warms
→ Solution: reduce CO₂ emissions
```

**Strength:** Simple, actionable, scientifically valid for the core mechanism.

**Limitation:** Treats carbon sinks as constant. Does not model the decline of fixation systems. Does not capture feedback loops. May lead to incomplete climate policy.

---

### Model 2: Multi-Cause System Model (This Framework)

```
Human activity
→ CO₂ emissions increase                          [Emission side]
→ Deforestation, soil degradation, ocean stress   [Pressure side]
→ Fixation systems decline                        [Sink side]
→ Natural circulation weakens                     [Circulation side]
→ Earth metabolism declines                       [Metabolic side]
→ CO₂ retention increases
→ Warming accelerates
→ Further fixation and circulation damage         [Feedback amplification]
```

**Strength:** Captures the full causal structure. Explains why CO₂ continues to rise even with moderate emission controls. Identifies additional intervention points (sink restoration, circulation support).

**Limitation:** More complex. Harder to quantify. Requires interdisciplinary data. Some pathways are empirically established; others remain hypotheses requiring verification (see HYPOTHESES_AND_VERIFICATION.md).

## Notes and Limitations

1. Causal arrows in this document represent hypothesized or empirically suggested relationships. Quantitative magnitudes are not specified here. Refer to HYPOTHESES_AND_VERIFICATION.md for verification status.

2. The causal structure is presented as a directed graph. In reality, many relationships are bidirectional and nonlinear. This document simplifies for clarity.

3. Node M-5 (Earth Metabolic Rate) is a conceptual aggregate, not yet a defined scientific measurement. It is included to represent the integrated decline of Earth's carbon-processing capacity.

4. Timescales vary widely across nodes. Some feedback loops operate on decadal to millennial scales; others on seasonal to annual scales. This document does not specify timescales.
